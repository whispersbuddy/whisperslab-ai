How it works

How does AI document processing work?

Four stages. The system handles the reading and filing, and a person stays in the loop for anything it is not sure about. Click a step for detail.

ConfidentUnsure
↑ Approved, then filed
Trigger AI step Rule Action Human review

Capture. Documents keep arriving the way they do today: an email attachment, a supplier portal download, a shared folder, a photo from a phone. Nobody on the sending side changes anything.

Read. The AI is given strict instructions for which fields to return and in what shape. It does not summarize or guess. It returns the same structure for every document of that type.

Check. Plain rules catch what AI should not be trusted with alone: line items that do not add up to the total, a vendor that is not on file, a date in the future.

File. Clean data is written to your system with a link back to the original document. Anything that failed a check waits for a person, and is filed the moment they approve it.

The honest part

What stays manual?

The system reads and files. It does not make judgment calls, and it should not. That is the part of the job that needed a person in the first place.

The system handles

  • Opening every document as it arrives
  • Pulling out the fields, line by line
  • Checking totals, dates, and known vendors
  • Filing clean data with a link to the original
  • Telling someone when a document needs eyes

A person still decides

  • Anything the system flagged as unsure
  • Approving payments and refunds
  • Disputes with vendors or clients
  • How unusual items are treated for tax
  • What to do when a number looks wrong

Clearing the air

Questions owners ask before they automate documents

What is AI document processing?+
AI document processing is software that reads business documents such as invoices, bank statements, receipts, and forms, pulls out specific fields like dates, amounts, and names, and writes them into the system where your team uses them. It replaces manual retyping. A well-built setup also checks its own output and sends anything it is unsure about to a person for review.
How much does AI document processing cost for a small business?+
At Whispers Lab it starts with a $250 Automation Audit that maps which documents cost your team the most time. If you go ahead, the build is quoted as a fixed price for the specific workflows, with no hourly billing, and the $250 is credited toward it. Ongoing maintenance and new workflows are available through the AI Growth Partner plan at $500 a month.
How long does it take to set up AI document processing?+
A document processing workflow built as part of the Whispers Lab Core Build is production-ready in under 30 days. That covers designing the data flow, building and connecting the automation, testing it against real sample documents, and training your team on how to review flagged items.
Is AI accurate enough to process financial documents?+
It is accurate enough when the system is built to be checked. The AI is told exactly which fields to return and in what format, plain validation rules confirm things like totals and dates, and any field that fails a check goes to a person instead of being filed. In the Financial Document Reader that Whispers Lab built for a financial services company, bank statement transactions are extracted in the same structure every time, which removed the manual data-entry step entirely.
Will AI document processing work with the software we already use?+
In most cases, yes. Extracted data can be written to accounting tools like Xero and QuickBooks, CRMs like HubSpot, Airtable or Google Sheets, or your own database. Documents can be collected from email inboxes, shared folders like Google Drive, or an upload form. The Whispers Lab Automation Audit confirms the exact connections before anything is built.
Does AI document processing replace bookkeepers or admin staff?+
No. AI document processing removes the retyping, not the judgment. Staff who used to enter data spend that time reviewing flagged documents, fixing real exceptions, and handling the work that needs a person, such as approving payments or answering a client's question about a number.
